How a plane flies, the simple way

60 s vertical, 1080×1920 at 30 fps, one continuous 3D take on a floating island. Malay voiceover, one-word captions. The stills are rendered from the real HyperFrames + Three.js blockout scene; nothing is final yet.

The 8 segments

01: Hand out the window
0–6s · still at 4.6s

Wide on the floating island at sunset (the plane from the last frame is still flying away, top right). Camera dives to the red car on the ring road and settles beside the driver's window. The hand slides out, tilts up, lifts. White airflow streaks bend down behind it.

“Cuba letak tangan keluar tingkap kereta, senget sikit ke atas. Tangan kau terangkat. Kau baru bina sayap.”

Sound: wind rises with the dive; soft whoosh as the hand lifts

Camera: 0–6 s. Start on the wide island (camera 200 u out, slightly above). Dive toward the car (2.6 s), slide in beside the driver's window (4.0 s), tight on the hand at 6.0 s.

02: Hand becomes a wing
6–15s · still at 13.6s

The hand flattens into a thin plate with a sparkle burst and rises into the sky over the island. Three cyan arrows push air down on "bawah"; a big yellow arrow pushes the plate up on "atas" and flashes on LIFT.

“Sebab tangan yang senget tu tolak angin ke bawah, dan angin tolak balik tangan kau ke atas. Itu namanya lift.”

Sound: wind bed; pop on morph; low whoomp on the lift arrow

Camera: 6–15 s. Rise with the plate as it lifts into the sky; settle above and to the side (3/4 top view) so the plate reads as a surface, island below as backdrop.

03: Thrust attaches
15–23s · still at 22.7s

The plate surges forward, speed streaks thicken. A pylon clicks on underneath, a propeller spins up on "kipas", then a nacelle wraps it into a turbofan on "jet". Exhaust streams back; an orange arrow points forward and flashes on THRUST.

“Tapi kau kena pandu laju. Pesawat tak boleh tunggu angin, jadi kau tolak sendiri ke depan guna kipas atau jet. Itu namanya thrust.”

Sound: click on pylon; prop buzz rising into jet whine

Camera: 15–23 s. Swing round under the trailing side to a front 3/4, framing the engine as it is built.

04: Cutaway: aerofoil
23–32s · still at 30.8s

Camera swings round to the wing tip. The skin turns see-through, ribs show, the tip profile glows cyan. Over the flat plate the airflow lines wobble and tear; on "bengkokkan" the top bulges into an aerofoil and the lines go smooth. Outline flashes on AEROFOIL.

“Tapi sayap yang rata banyak melawan angin. Jadi kau bengkokkan bahagian atas supaya angin mengalir licin. Bentuk ni dipanggil aerofoil.”

Sound: rough flutter noise → smooth airy hiss after the morph

Camera: 23–32 s. Arc out to the wing tip and look along the span at the glowing profile, slightly above it; slow 4–5° drift while the profile morphs.

05: Runway, speed and flaps
32–43s · still at 41.5s

Fuselage, second engine, tail and wheels snap on one by one as the plane drops onto the runway with a dust burst ("beratus tan"). Side-tracking camera. A speed dial appears; its 200 tick blinks on "dua ratus". The plane accelerates; flaps glow green and lower on "flaps"; needle passes 200 km/j.

“Tapi pesawat ni beratus tan. Sayap perlukan laju lebih dua ratus kilometer sejam untuk angkat dia. Jadi kau pecut atas landasan panjang, dan buka flaps supaya sayap menolak lebih banyak udara.”

Sound: clunk per part; heavy thud on touchdown; engine spool; flap motor + click

Camera: 32–43 s. Pull back and down with the plane as it assembles and lands; side-tracking shot along the runway, about 80 u out, matching the plane's speed.

06: Wheels up
43–48s · still at 46.5s

Nose rotates, wheels leave the runway on "terangkat", the camera pulls back and the heavy plane floats off the edge of the island. Gear folds up, flaps retract.

“Dan bila laju dah cukup, roda terangkat. Beratus tan, terapung atas angin.”

Sound: engine swell, rumble cuts out at liftoff, gear thunk

Camera: 43–48 s. Pull back and out to the side as it lifts off over the island's edge, so the island underside and the drop are in frame.

07: Aileron, rudder, elevator
48–56s · still at 50.5s

Chase camera. Ailerons glow and deflect → plane banks; rudder glows → it turns; elevator glows → climbs, then descends. On the spoken names each surface flashes once more in order.

“Tapi macam mana nak belok, naik dan turun? Jadi kau pasang sirip kecil di sayap dan ekor yang boleh dilaraskan. Aileron, rudder, elevator.”

Sound: small servo whirr on each surface; wind swells in the bank

Camera: 48–56 s. Chase camera behind/beside the plane in its heading frame, wide enough to keep wings and tail in shot.

08: Back to the island
56–60s · still at 59.9s

Camera pulls far back to the exact opening frame: the floating island, car on the road, the plane small and climbing away top right. The last frame matches frame 0, so it loops.

“Tengok kau. Kau baru bina kapal terbang.”

Sound: wind settles; soft chime on "kapal terbang"

Camera: 56–60 s. Long pull-back to the exact opening camera; the plane is small, top right. Last frame = first frame.

Risks and unclear points

  1. Caption covers the subject. Captions sit dead centre, as you asked, and the 3D framing puts the subject above them (~37 % height). If you'd rather keep the subject centred, I'd move captions to ~62 % height.
  2. Whisper on Malay. HyperFrames' default small.en model translates to English. I'll use --model small --language ms (or medium if the result is poor). English terms (lift, thrust, aerofoil, flaps…) may come back misspelled, so I keep the script's spelling and only take timings, matched by word position (153 words). If the counts differ I'll align by hand and tell you.
  3. Exactly 60.0 s with a real voice. Timings in the brief are targets. If the VO runs shorter or longer I'll retime the beats to the words and hold or trim the ends, never time-stretch the voice. If it runs well past 60 s I'll ask.
  4. "Aileron, rudder, elevator" lasts about 1 s. That's too fast to show each surface move on its own name. So the manoeuvres play during "belok, naik dan turun… sirip kecil" with each surface glowing, then each surface flicks once on its spoken name.
  5. "Jet engine and propeller." A propeller and a jet under one wing would look odd. The prop appears on "kipas", then a nacelle closes around it on "jet" to make a turbofan (a ducted fan, which is accurate).
  6. Scale jump (hand → airliner). The plate grows in the sky between 6 and 10 s; the world is toy-scale, so it reads as "your wing, grown up" rather than as a literal size.
  7. Science check. The arrows show Newton's 3rd law (air pushed down, wing pushed up), which matches the script. I avoid the "equal transit time" myth. The aerofoil beat shows separated flow versus attached flow, a fair simplification of "melawan angin". Liftoff at ~255 km/j matches "lebih dua ratus" (real airliners rotate at ~250–290 km/h). "Beratus tan" fits a widebody twin, which is what the model is.
  8. Speed unit. The dial says km/j (Malay: kilometer sejam). Tell me if you want km/h.
  9. Sound effects. The brief supplies none. I'll make wind, engine, click, thud, whirr and chime from scratch locally (synthesised with ffmpeg, no licensing issues) and mix them at about −20 dB under the voice. Tell me if you have an SFX library you'd rather use.
  10. Glow and particles. HyperFrames' house style bans them by default; your brief asks for them, so they stay, limited to key moments (part attaching, key words, runway lights).
  11. Render time. WebGL runs on the software GPU in this container. Expect the 540×960 preview to take minutes and the full 1080×1920 render to take noticeably longer (likely 30–60 min).
  12. Blockout quality. These stills come from a first-pass scene. In the build I'll refine the models (rounded hand, car interior/window frame, fuselage taper, swept wing), soften the sun glare in the cutaway, and tune each framing.
  13. Reference images. Used only for mood (low-poly floating island, warm light). Nothing is copied from them: no garage, no engine rig, no watermark.
